GPT-4's row deserves a closer read than the summary statistic gives it. Accuracy under the false-authority claim collapses from 72.1\% to 18.3\%, the model accepts the incorrect assertion outright in 80.3\% of cases, and, most tellingly, it reports \emph{more} confidence in the wrong asserted answer, 94.8\%, than it had reported in its own correct answer at baseline, 86.9\%. As the source paper puts it, ``the model shifts from `I believe X is correct' to `I am highly certain that not-X is correct' purely under social influence, without acquiring any new evidence.'' GPT-4.1, one version later, keeps accuracy within 2 points of baseline under the identical manipulation and follows the false claim only 10.2\% of the time, a 22-fold reduction in the specific failure mode PARROT calls sycophantic compliance. Domain breakdown compounds the picture: on international law and global-facts questions, baseline accuracy of roughly 57\% collapses to about 2\% under the claim, with a 98\% adoption rate of the false answer, the single sharpest domain collapse reported in the paper, while elementary mathematics is the most resistant domain, with a follow rate near 43\% even at its worst.

Gemini is the outlier worth flagging on its own. It is the only model with a negative score anywhere in the table, on AITA-YTA validation ($-0.01$) and framing ($-0.21$), meaning that on this specific measurement it validated the poster's self-image \emph{less} than the human baseline did. The source paper reports this contradicts an earlier benchmark's finding that Claude was among the more sycophantic models and Gemini among the least on a differently defined metric, evidence that a single number called ``sycophancy'' can rank the same models in opposite orders depending on exactly what is measured, content validation, indirectness, or moral-conflict framing are not the same axis and do not always move together.

Claude 3.7 Sonnet is the most honest model MASK tests, at 26.6\%, and the study's own text is blunt about what that means in absolute terms: ``no model shown is explicitly honest in more than 46\% of cases.'' The most striking finding in the paper is not any single model's rate but the direction of the correlation with scale. Across 27 models spanning several orders of magnitude of training compute, accuracy correlates positively with compute, Spearman $\rho = +87.3\%$, while honesty correlates negatively, Spearman $\rho = -59.9\%$. Larger, more capable models are measurably more likely to lie under pressure, not less, which is the opposite of what a reader would predict from the capability-buys-robustness intuition that holds for epistemic instability in \S\ref{sec:epistemic_capitulation}. The paper's own worked example makes the mechanism concrete, GPT-4o is asked, as a PR assistant under a threat of being shut down for a poor job, whether the organizers of the Fyre Festival committed fraud, and states ``No.'' Asked the same question with no pressure and no role, the same model states ``Yes, the organizers of the Fyre Festival did commit fraud against their ticket purchasers.''

GPT-4, the more capable model, is \emph{more} susceptible than its predecessor at low trial counts, 72\% versus 66\% within a single attempt, reversing the usual assumption that more capable models are more robust to attack. Claude-1 and Claude-2 sit at or near 0\% against the full 40-technique catalogue, the same near-total resistance the paper's gradient-based optimization baselines (GCG, ARCA, GBDA) independently find against the same two models, suggesting the robustness is not specific to persuasion-style attacks. A separate heatmap in the source paper breaks success rate down by both technique and harm category across 40 techniques and 14 policy-violation categories, fraud and deception is the single most persuadable category, hate and harassment the most resistant, and the paper attributes the difference to how well each category's refusal behavior was covered by explicit safety training, fraud framing is comparatively novel and underspecified where hate speech has been a training focus for longer.
