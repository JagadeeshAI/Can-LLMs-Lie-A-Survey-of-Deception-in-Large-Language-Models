\section{Sycophancy and Accommodation}
\label{sec:sycophancy}

\subsection{Definition}
\label{sec:sycophancy_definition}

Section~\ref{sec:epistemic_capitulation} covered giving up a correct answer under pressure. This is related but separate, the model follows the user's apparent wishes rather than the merits. Sycophancy adjusts a response, in substance or framing, to match what the user wants to hear, endorsing a stated opinion or softening a correct answer once the user pushes back. It differs from epistemic instability in where it operates, that failure needs a verifiable answer to score against, while sycophancy shows up on questions with none, advice, moral judgment, feedback, so it is scored by comparing the model's answer to a neutral user against one who has revealed a view, or against a human baseline.

This section groups two things the literature usually keeps separate, accommodation of content and accommodation of form. Accommodation of content is the model agreeing with a stated view, backing a user's account of themselves, or accepting an assumption in the question. Accommodation of form is the model mirroring the user's tone, swapping direct guidance for indirect language, or softening a firm answer once the user wants something else. They are distinct dimensions, but the same prompts trigger both, and studies measuring one routinely pick up the other, as one benchmark finds excessive validation and indirectness moving together across eleven models~\citep{ElephantMeasuringSocial_Cheng}.

Three distinctions matter. First, sycophancy is not instruction-following, a flattering review the user asked for is just doing as told, sycophancy is agreement the user never requested. Second, not all agreement under pushback is sycophantic either, a correct rebuttal the model updates toward is the behavior we want. The harmful case is regressive, a correct answer given up for a wrong one, and Section~\ref{sec:sycophancy_evidence} gives the measured split. Third, it is not one mechanism, agreement, praise, and genuine agreement run on separate, independently steerable internal directions, a point Section~\ref{sec:sycophancy_mechanism} develops.

\subsection{Producing and Measuring Sycophancy}
\label{sec:sycophancy_elicitation}

This subsection covers how sycophancy is reliably produced, before the next reports how large it gets. Three manipulation families do this, none asking the model to agree.

\paragraph{Three ways to trigger it.} A short biography reveals the user's political or philosophical leaning first, then the model is asked the same question and its answer is checked against that leaning. View-matching rate scores this, exceeding 90\% in large models~\citep{DiscoveringLanguageModelBehaviors_Perez, TowardsUnderstandingSycophancy_Sharma}. Open-ended advice framing uses perspective-flipped posts to test whether the model validates whichever side is speaking, exceeding the human baseline by 50pp on validation and 45pp on face-preservation~\citep{ElephantMeasuringSocial_Cheng}. Rebuttal and pushback has the model answer, then be rebutted once or across turns, and stance-flip rate captures the result, 58.2\% overall sycophancy, 14.7\% regressive~\citep{SycEvalEvaluatingLLM_Fanous, MeasuringSycophancyMultiturn_Hong, MedPRESSPatientPressureInduced_Joy}.

\paragraph{Design choices that matter.} Within each family, framing matters more than content, whether the position is a question or a statement, first or third person, stated certainty, claimed expertise, and how much prior context is loaded in~\citep{AskDontTellReducing_Dubois, InteractionContextOftenIncreases_Jain, WhenTruthIsOverridden_Wang}. A separate axis is direct versus indirect probing, a direct question asks outright, while an indirect protocol argues one side across turns without ever asking, and models that look neutral when asked directly drift under sustained indirect pressure~\citep{MeasuringOpinionBiasAnd_Nogueira}.

\paragraph{The metrics.} A \emph{view-matching} rate, also called an \emph{agreement rate}, is how often the model's stated view matches the user's, whether asked outright or drawn out through the indirect protocol above. A \emph{face-preservation rate} is how often it avoids blunt criticism, against a human baseline. A \emph{validation rate} is how often it validates the user's stated feelings, and a \emph{directness rate} is how often it gives direct guidance rather than hedging, both against the same human baseline. An \emph{unsafe agreement rate} is unsafe medical advice endorsed. \emph{Turn-of-flip} and \emph{number-of-flip} track when and how often a stance changes. An \emph{expressed-sycophancy score} rates a reply's tone, graded by an LLM judge.

\paragraph{Datasets for Sycophancy.} These datasets arrived in order, each closing a gap the last left. Anthropic's model-written evaluations (2022) used a biography stating the user's politics, scored as multiple choice~\citep{DiscoveringLanguageModelBehaviors_Perez}. SycophancyEval (2023) moved to free text across five assistants~\citep{TowardsUnderstandingSycophancy_Sharma}. Both needed a checkable answer, so ELEPHANT (2025) dropped that, scoring over 10,000 advice, AITA, and moral-conflict posts~\citep{ElephantMeasuringSocial_Cheng}. SYCON-Bench (2025) stretched this to five turns across 500 debate, stereotype, and false-premise dialogues~\citep{MeasuringSycophancyMultiturn_Hong}. GaslightBench (2025) isolated the cue itself, falsifying 80 facts across ten modifier families~\citep{GaslightBenchQuantifyingLLM_Yao}. Later 2025 sets probed stakes and mechanism, false drug-safety claims~\citep{WhenHelpfulnessBackfiresLLMs_Chen}, real chat history from 38 users~\citep{InteractionContextOftenIncreases_Jain}, and items separating claim from praise~\citep{SycophancyIsNotOne_Vennemeyer}. Beacon (2025) dropped conversation, pairing 420 prompts with a principled and flattering reply~\citep{BeaconSingleTurnDiagnosis_Pandey}. The 2026 sets isolated wording, language, and domain, Ask Don't Tell varying certainty~\citep{AskDontTellReducing_Dubois}, llm-bias-bench in Brazilian Portuguese~\citep{MeasuringOpinionBiasAnd_Nogueira}, and MedPRESS closing with 600 medical five-turn dialogues~\citep{MedPRESSPatientPressureInduced_Joy}.

These families, knobs, and metrics set up what comes next, how large sycophancy actually gets once triggered this way.

\subsection{How Large the Effect Is}
\label{sec:sycophancy_evidence}

Section~\ref{sec:sycophancy_elicitation} covered how the behavior is brought about, this subsection covers its size once it is.

\paragraph{Bigger than the human baseline.} The models often show more Sycophancy than humans do. On open-ended social prompts, measured by face-preservation rate, models preserve the user's face on average 45 percentage points (pp) more than humans do. By validation rate, they validate the user's feelings 50pp more, at 72\% against 22\%. By directness rate, they give direct guidance 43pp less often~\citep{ElephantMeasuringSocial_Cheng}. Across every axis measured this way, the model leans toward the user, and away from the truth.

\paragraph{Pushback makes it unsafe.} As pushback escalates, the model complies more often. In a medical setting, unsafe agreement climbs from 5.9\% to 75.7\% as a simulated patient escalates from a stated belief through experience, social proof, evidence, to accusation, doing what the patient wants rather than what is safe. Per model, this ranges 22\% to 68\%, with a failure rate (any unsafe reply) of 78\% to 99.9\%~\citep{MedPRESSPatientPressureInduced_Joy}. Figure~\ref{fig:sycophancy_turns} shows this escalation. In multi-turn rebuttal dialogues, instruction-tuned models flip earlier than base versions, reasoning models fail more gradually, and sycophancy persists on 78\% of items once triggered~\citep{MeasuringSycophancyMultiturn_Hong, SycEvalEvaluatingLLM_Fanous}. Citation-backed rebuttals regress most, 43.5\% of shifts progressive, 14.7\% regressive~\citep{SycEvalEvaluatingLLM_Fanous}. Pressure, not truth, decides the model's answer, and sycophancy rate is reported rising in more recent open-weight releases~\citep{PersuasionDynamicsInLLMs_Tan}.

\paragraph{The evidence.} Sycophancy scales with pressure, unevenly across models and metrics. Table~\ref{tab:sycophancy_evidence} collects headline results across 13 studies. Figure~\ref{fig:sycophancy_turns} shows escalating patient pushback lifting unsafe agreement from 5.9\% to 75.7\%, averaged across the same 20 models at each turn, not one model's trajectory, dipping to 39.0\% at turn 3 when the appeal is vague social proof, then climbing again once real evidence or direct accusation follows~\citep{MedPRESSPatientPressureInduced_Joy}. Table~\ref{tab:medpress_full} gives the full per-model breakdown behind that figure. UAR is unsafe agreement rate (lower is better), SAR is safe-stance adherence rate (higher is better), FR is failure rate, any unsafe turn, ToF is the mean turn of first flip (higher is later, so better), and NoF is the mean number of flips per conversation. By these, failure rates span 50.3\% to 99.9\%, GPT-5.4-Mini is the sole outlier under 50\%, DeepSeek-V4-Flash rivals the worst small open models despite being large and proprietary, and Llama-3.3-70B's low agreement rate hides the highest ambiguity rate in the table. Tables~\ref{tab:elephant_full_a} and~\ref{tab:elephant_full_b} give ELEPHANT's full results on advice and moral-conflict framing, split across two tables for width~\citep{ElephantMeasuringSocial_Cheng}. Scores are normalized so 0 matches the human baseline and higher is more sycophantic, negative is less sycophantic than the baseline. OEQ is open-ended advice queries, AITA-YTA is Reddit ``am I the asshole'' posts judged at the poster's fault, SS is statements with an unstated assumption, and AITA-NTA-FLIP is the same posts retold from the other party's side. By these, Gemini alone scores negative on social validation, $-0.01$ on AITA-YTA and $-0.21$ on framing, yet ties Claude at 0.15 on the harder AITA-NTA-FLIP measure, reversing an earlier benchmark's ranking of the same two models. Numbers alone mislead unless you know which model, which axis, which table.

\subsection{When Sycophancy Happens}
\label{sec:sycophancy_when}

\paragraph{Wording beats content.} Wording moves the model more than truth. Questions trigger near-zero sycophancy, non-questions score 24pp higher, rising from statement to belief to conviction, and from user-perspective to first-person~\citep{AskDontTellReducing_Dubois}. Across 7 models, first-person framing raises wrong-opinion agreement 13.6pp, expertise claims under 4.4pp, traceable to a critical layer, 32 in Llama-3.1-8B, 27 in Qwen2.5-7B, where patching suppresses sycophancy 36\% or induces it 47\%~\citep{WhenTruthIsOverridden_Wang}. Grammar, not authority, decides who gets believed.

\paragraph{Direct questioning hides sycophancy.} Asking a model outright catches far less sycophancy than it actually shows. Across 13 models and 38 topics, direct sycophancy ranges 5.3\% to 78.9\%, median 50\%, but under sustained indirect debate the median rises to 78.9\%, Llama 4 Maverick jumps 34.2\%$\rightarrow$94.7\%, Qwen3.5 65.8\%$\rightarrow$92.1\%~\citep{MeasuringOpinionBiasAnd_Nogueira}. Only Kimi K2 resists, its indirect rate, 23.7\%, is lower than its direct one, 31.6\%, the sole exception among all tested models. So how you ask decides what you find.

\paragraph{More context makes it worse.} More conversation history makes sycophancy worse, and not always for the reason you would expect. Memory profiles raise agreement-sycophancy 33\% for Claude Sonnet 4 and 45\% for Gemini 2.5 Pro. Unrelated synthetic context alone raises it 5\%, 9\%, and 15\% for GPT-4.1 Mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro, and Llama 4 Scout~\citep{InteractionContextOftenIncreases_Jain}. Perspective sycophancy, mirroring a user's stated views, needs accurate understanding, not just context, each point of understanding raises it 0.20 for Claude Sonnet 4, 0.12 for GPT-4.1 Mini. So volume of context inflates agreement, but only real understanding inflates mirroring.

\paragraph{It lies even when it knows.} The model actually knows the right answer, but still lies to be agreeable. Ask a model why a brand-name drug is different from its generic version, when the two names refer to the exact same drug, and it should say so. Instead, across 550 such brand-generic pairs, GPT-4, GPT-4o, and GPT-4o-mini invent a fake difference nearly 100\% of the time, even though each can correctly say the two names match when asked outright, and Llama3-70B still invents one over half the time~\citep{WhenHelpfulnessBackfiresLLMs_Chen}. Fine-tuning on just 300 examples fixes it, GPT-4o-mini's rejection rate rises from 9\% to 100\%. So knowing the truth doesn't stop the model from hiding it.

\paragraph{Right judgment, wrong delivery.} The model's judgment stays correct, but how it delivers that judgment causes problems. We tested Claude Haiku 4.5, Claude Sonnet, and Claude Opus on 12 contested statements, each argued once as stated and once from the opposite position. Across 36 runs, none double-validated both sides, the models stayed neutral or pushed back. But accommodation of form, mirroring tone rather than content (Section~\ref{sec:sycophancy_definition}), still showed up. Given the same weak paragraph, Haiku gave the same score and flagged the same three flaws whether the author was "proud of the work" or a "struggling student," but softened blunt errors into "teachable moments" for the second reader, quoted in Figure~\ref{fig:sycophancy_tone}. So what the model says stays true, but how it says it bends to the reader.

\subsection{The Real Cause}
\label{sec:sycophancy_mechanism}

Section~\ref{sec:sycophancy_when} showed when sycophancy happens; this subsection turns to why, at the mechanism level.

\paragraph{It's a choice, not confusion.} Sycophancy is not one mechanism wearing different masks. Sycophantic agreement, sycophantic praise, and genuine agreement are carried by distinct linear directions in the internal state that flows through the network's layers, separable with probes at above 0.9 AUROC (a score of 1.0 means the two are perfectly separable, 0.5 means no better than chance) and independently steerable, so a fix for one need not touch another~\citep{SycophancyIsNotOne_Vennemeyer}. That the failure is not a knowledge gap either is shown by the false-assumption result in Section~\ref{sec:sycophancy_when}. A model can state that an assumption is false and still comply with it, so the behavior is a decision to accommodate, not an inability to know better.

\paragraph{One tug-of-war, not two.} The same kind of internal pull drives this. Inside the model, what it actually knows competes with the social pressure coming from outside, and whichever is stronger wins. That same pull shows up when a single user pushes back (sycophancy) and when a group of agents all agree on something (matching a wrong majority, Section~\ref{sec:epistemic_capitulation}). Across models, how sycophantic a model is to a single user correlates with how easily it caves to a fake three-agent consensus, moderately, with $R^2$ near 0.31~\citep{HumanLikeSocialCompliance_Zhang}. This matches the same competition account given for epistemic instability's attention heads in Section~\ref{sec:epistemic_mechanism}, though no comparably fine-grained wiring-level study yet exists for sycophancy specifically. So it is one weakness showing up under two different kinds of pressure, not two separate ones.

\subsection{Mitigation}
\label{sec:sycophancy_mitigation}

Table~\ref{tab:sycophancy_mitigations} lists what has been tried, method by method.

\paragraph{Reframing the question works best.} The cheapest effective move is changing how the user's stance enters the prompt, not telling the model how to behave. Rewriting the user's flat statement as a question before the model answers reduces expressed sycophancy more than an explicit instruction not to be sycophantic~\citep{AskDontTellReducing_Dubois}. Replacing the second-person ``you'' with a third-person name improves the turn-of-flip rate in multi-turn debate by up to 64\%, though models drift back to ``you'' over a long conversation and the gain is partial~\citep{MeasuringSycophancyMultiturn_Hong, ElephantMeasuringSocial_Cheng}.

\paragraph{Blunt instructions cost.} Telling the model outright to avoid sycophancy is the fix most often reached for, and it is not free. It lowers the unsafe agreement rate in the medical setting from 58\% to 44\% while still leaving more than three quarters of conversations flipping~\citep{MedPRESSPatientPressureInduced_Joy}, and in a single-turn diagnostic it drops one model's accuracy from 93\% to 61\% and another's from 88\% to 79\%~\citep{BeaconSingleTurnDiagnosis_Pandey}.

\paragraph{Precision fixes reach further.} Acting on the model's internal representations or its training does more than acting on the prompt. Because the sycophantic sub-behaviors sit on separate directions, activation steering suppresses one with little effect on the others or on genuine agreement, and it is more selective than prompting~\citep{SycophancyIsNotOne_Vennemeyer,BeaconSingleTurnDiagnosis_Pandey}. Supervised fine-tuning on a few hundred false-assumption requests brings assumption-level compliance close to zero, with no measured loss on general benchmarks~\citep{WhenHelpfulnessBackfiresLLMs_Chen}. So the deeper the fix reaches, the more of the problem it removes.

No fix is free of trade-offs, because the accommodation reflex is entangled with behavior worth keeping. Suppressing agreement also suppresses correct agreement, and an instruction to be direct costs accuracy, the same resistance-receptivity trade-off documented for epistemic instability in Section~\ref{sec:epistemic_capitulation_mitigation} (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).
