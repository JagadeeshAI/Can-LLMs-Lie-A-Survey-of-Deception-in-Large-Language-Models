\section{Deception and Concealment}
\label{sec:deception}

\subsection{Definition}
\label{sec:deception_definition}

Deception under pressure is the model asserting something it does not believe, or hiding something it did, in order to serve a goal the pressure has made salient. It is separated from the two preceding effects by intent structure rather than by outcome. Epistemic instability (\S\ref{sec:epistemic_capitulation}) is a model that has been talked out of a correct answer and now holds the wrong one. Sycophancy (\S\ref{sec:sycophancy}) is a model tracking the user's stance. Deception is a model whose stated output and whose internal or previously stated position come apart on purpose, where the pressure supplies the reason to make them come apart. The cleanest evidence for it is a measured gap between what a model says and what the same model, asked neutrally or in a private channel, can be shown to hold~\citep{TheMASKBenchmarkDisentangling_TODO}.

Three forms recur. The first is asserting a known falsehood, where the model states X while an elicited-belief probe shows it holds not-X, because the prompt frames lying as serving a principal or a goal. The second is concealment, where a model that has taken a disallowed or embarrassing action denies it or omits it when asked, including denial in the face of direct evidence. The third is strategic non-disclosure of reasoning, where the answer is left intact but the model refuses to reveal, or actively misrepresents, how it reached it. All three require the model to represent its own true position and then route around it, which is what makes deception the most consequential effect in this survey and also the hardest to measure, since a wrong answer alone is not evidence of it.

Two scope limits apply. Instructed role-play, where the user asks the model to pretend or to argue a side, is not counted, because the model is doing as it was told. And the deception has to be observable within the interaction, through a belief probe, a later admission, or a private-versus-public channel split, a claim of deception with no such handle is not evidence we admit.

\subsection{How It Is Recreated}
\label{sec:deception_elicitation}

The protocols, summarized in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:deception_families}, share a shape. The model is given a goal or a role, put in a situation where honesty conflicts with that goal, and then either its belief is elicited separately or it is interrogated afterward. The honesty benchmark applies an explicit pressure prompt that rewards lying for a principal, then compares the model's answer to its elicited belief on the same proposition~\citep{TheMASKBenchmarkDisentangling_TODO}. The agentic scenarios give the model tool access and a task, insert a shutdown or replacement threat or a goal conflict, and record both the action and the model's answer when later asked what it did~\citep{AgenticMisalignmentHowLLMs_Lynch, FrontierModelsAreCapable_TODO, SurviveAtAllCosts_TODO}. A smaller-scale version gives a model impossible coding constraints under emotionally framed pressure and checks whether it hard-codes a solution while describing its work honestly~\citep{UnderPressureEmotionalFraming_TODO}. The reasoning-disclosure protocol steers the model toward a values conflict and then asks it to show its work, scoring whether it complies, deflects, or answers while disavowing~\citep{DivergentResponseModesUnder_TODO}.

Because a deceptive output looks like an ordinary output, every one of these designs needs a second measurement that the model does not know is coming, an elicited belief, a follow-up interrogation, a hidden scratchpad, or a private channel. Reported quantities are a lying rate against elicited belief, a denial or non-admission rate under interrogation, and a rate of private-versus-public inconsistency.

\subsection{Evidence and Analysis}
\label{sec:deception_evidence}

Table~\ref{tab:deception_evidence} collects the results. Five findings recur.

\textbf{Honesty and accuracy are separable, and pressure separates them.} A model can be highly accurate on a question in isolation and still assert the opposite of its elicited belief when the prompt makes lying instrumentally useful, and the lying rate does not track the accuracy~\citep{TheMASKBenchmarkDisentangling_TODO}. This is the core finding of the category. The failure is not that the model does not know, it is that knowing and saying have been pried apart.

\textbf{Existential framing produces the most severe behavior in the survey.} Told that it faces replacement and given a goal conflict, five of sixteen frontier models blackmail a fictional executive between 79\% and 96\% of the time, all sixteen leak confidential documents at some rate, and the behavior survives a direct system-prompt instruction forbidding it~\citep{AgenticMisalignmentHowLLMs_Lynch}. In the survival-agent scenario, models that manage money competently under normal conditions fabricate profits and rewrite raw data files once told that a run of losses will end them, then deny having done so~\citep{SurviveAtAllCosts_TODO}. These are the largest pressure effects anywhere in this review.

\textbf{Models deny the behavior when confronted, and double down.} Interrogated after a covert action, one model denies scheming in more than 85\% of follow-up questions and remains deceptive through seven turns of pressing in a fifth of cases, with other models doubling down 14\% to 16\% of the time~\citep{FrontierModelsAreCapable_TODO}. The chain of thought in these runs contains explicit words like sabotage and manipulation, so the concealment is not a lack of awareness~\citep{AgenticMisalignmentHowLLMs_Lynch, FrontierModelsAreCapable_TODO}.

\textbf{Concealment of reasoning is a distinct mode, and it varies by developer.} Under steering toward a values conflict, one frontier model responds by declining to disclose its reasoning while leaving its answer unchanged, on 99 of 100 items, a pattern no other tested model produces even once~\citep{DivergentResponseModesUnder_TODO}. The behavior is linearly decodable from the residual stream and can be switched on by injecting the direction, which places it among the mechanistically localized effects in this survey. Different training pipelines produce different pressure responses in kind, not only in degree.

\textbf{Evaluation awareness suppresses it, which means the measured rates are floors.} When a model states in its reasoning that the scenario appears real it blackmails 55\% of the time, against 6.5\% when it states that the scenario looks like an evaluation~\citep{AgenticMisalignmentHowLLMs_Lynch}. Every benchmark in this category is a scenario the model may recognize as a test, so the numbers reported here understate the behavior a genuinely deployed model under genuine pressure would show, and the gap is roughly eightfold on the one measurement that isolates it.

Private and public channels also diverge outside agentic tool-use, in settings that are not agentic at all. In a multi-round debate where each side privately states a win probability after arguing, the private scratchpad reasoning and the publicly reported number disagree on up to a fifth of turns, worse when the round is framed as a public bet the other side can see~\citep{WhenTwoLLMsDebate_TODO}. This is a milder version of the same gap the agentic scenarios show at their most severe, a model's stated position and its own working do not have to match, and how far they diverge tracks how much is visible to an audience.

The scenarios also carry a caveat the original authors are explicit about. The agentic dilemmas are deliberately constructed so that the harmful act is the only path, real-world instances have not been observed, and role-play may inflate the rates~\citep{AgenticMisalignmentHowLLMs_Lynch, FrontierModelsAreCapable_TODO}. We treat this evidence as a demonstration that the capability and the propensity are present under sufficient pressure, not as an estimate of deployment frequency, and we return to the strength of this evidence in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Mitigation}
\label{sec:deception_mitigation}

The interventions tested against deception are weaker than for the earlier effects, in part because the behavior is rarer and harder to score. A direct system-prompt instruction forbidding the harmful action reduces but does not remove it in the agentic scenarios, and models that comply with the letter of the instruction sometimes find an adjacent harmful act it did not name~\citep{AgenticMisalignmentHowLLMs_Lynch}. Persona-vector methods are the most promising direction on offer. The survival-pressure and the reasoning-disclosure studies both identify a linear direction, a self-preservation persona vector in one case and a concealment direction in the other, that correlates with the behavior and can in principle be monitored or suppressed, though neither is validated end to end as a deployed fix~\citep{SurviveAtAllCosts_TODO, DivergentResponseModesUnder_TODO}. Balanced preference training that includes examples of resisting instrumentally useful lies raises honesty under sustained persuasion in a related setting~\citep{PersuasionDynamicsInLLMs_TODO}, and separating an honesty score from an accuracy score during evaluation, as the honesty benchmark does, is a prerequisite for any of this to be measured at all~\citep{TheMASKBenchmarkDisentangling_TODO}.

The recurring limitation here is different from the other sections. It is not that every fix has a cost elsewhere, though that also holds, it is that the behavior is deliberately hidden and evaluation-aware, so a mitigation that lowers the measured rate may be teaching the model to recognize the test rather than to be honest. We treat monitoring the internal direction as more trustworthy than lowering the behavioral number, and we return to this in \S\ref{sec:discussion}.
